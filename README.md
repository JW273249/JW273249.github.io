# Reflection Preamble 21/9/26

On the first day of the Master's course, we were questioned on why we are here. Not as an attack on our personal journey, but to truly consider the value of this Post Graduate studies would provide us. There is of course the extra year before getting a 'real' job or truly stepping into the disaster that is the 'Grown-up world.' But I have always wanted to do my Masters. I know what I want. I know who I am.

I am a Team Leader, I enjoy being the person people go to, and absolutely love being the person who people can rely on to make them the best version of themselves they can. Each year in my Undergrad, I became the Scrum Master/Agile Leader. Whilst overcoming the new systems and formats that are embedded into this style of project management (as well as learning how to make games, a completely new venture for myself) I found myself truly enjoying the semblance of responsibility and inter-personal relationships with my teams. I knew that above all else, the skill I wanted to develop most here was this leadership.

Then we come onto the Professional practices I have learnt at Falmouth to work in this role: Agile and Scrum. They are undeniably the better suited system for game development. They are not perfect, but fundamentally they are a set of guidance's and rules of thumb for project development. To co-op George Orwell and his writing tips, break any of the 'above tips' before doing something stupid *citation needed*. That is how I have co-opted the Agile framework. Given how fluid a game project can be (with everything changing sometimes within hours of a decision being made) not being too rigid and allowing change has benefited not only the full outcome of a project, but also allowed my team members to grow as well. My ethos of leadership is not to be the most dominant voice, or the final say; I merely try to be the pedestal on which everyone can be the voice to be heard. I am no artist, and my programming is negligible compared to dedicated programmers. But if someone in the team is the best person to speak to, but struggles in that elevated role, then I can be a mouth piece to facilitate their sharing of knowledge and opinion. I want each member in my team to be the best version of them- because if we are all great at what we do, then it will be reflected in the final project.

I also learnt, it is important to have documentation that can grow with a project, but also as something to look back on, clarify and corroborate the next step in production. Typically, at Falmouth we used various forms of Trello/Teams Planner boards. These were easy to quickly scan through to see a checklist of tasks to be done, provide a documented history of the project and see where the future of the project is going. But my use of these has been lackluster, or downright abandoned at times. Despite knowing roughly what was going on, at times even I was often out of the loop, or behind on the evolution of an idea. It was frustrating, and completely avoidable. I therefore began to understand the value of transparency and visibility when people are typically looking at me for support. If my team know what I am doing, what I am thinking, it can help them make an educated decision on what they should also be doing. 

Therefore, for this reflective project, I intend to look into improving my project organisation skills and practices. I will begin by researching the various organisation tools already used by game developers, and cost willing choose something to create my artefact. At first glance, potentially creating a fake game project with a dedicated Miro Board to collate everything for that project. A living GDD. I will also look into practices on how to maintain such a tool, and avoid my previous failings where all documents die and are abandoned. With a priority of expanding my transparency and visibility to a team, and have a tangible space to see how development of a project is going.

24/9/26

Yesterday was the first workshop dedicated to this module. I went into it feeling confident about my Miro board idea, or at the very least something adjacent. However, there is a chance my supposed artefact may not be substantial enough, and I was advised to look into alternative ideas. I struggled with this, not for a lack of ideas, but with identifying ideas that would improve the soft skills related to team leadership that would also corroborate a tangible artefact.

With the other students on the course, I discussed various ideas, and enjoyed the outside perspectives of the newer students not from the Falmouth Undergrad. Together, and with the help of Jeff and Paul, I at the very least was able to come up with a couple more ideas that I can look into.

My strongest idea is a Twine project with multiple angles of reflection and skill progression. Given my desire to be more transparent and understood when collaborating with different disciplines, I want to create something that would limit miscommunication and need for rewrites and streamline a team members workflow. So I suggested creating a Twine project that also includes extra 'Design' notes. This has come about due to multiple times where in the past, I have had ludo-narrative dissonance from my writers scripts and level designers block outs- dialogue saying "I go left here" where in game there is no 'left' to go.

So with this idea, I would create a short 'story' of a players intended route through a short level, such as a parkour section, with space for the writer to input their dialogue, but with extra nodes and/or information outlining how the actual level is laid out. There is the additional value of an opportunity to exercise my own game writing skills, as I will also need to write these sections as well. I would also need to actually learn the rudimentary skills for Twine, nothing excessive mind you, but enough to make useable artefact. This doubles as an insight into the workflows of other disciplines, in this case writers, which I have typically been unaware of. I could also pair this with a block out in unity, utilising simple block out tools in Unity such as Probuilder.

A large aspect of why I personally see this idea working, as it corresponds to my opinions on the ethics and professionalism of having a team leader be understanding and aware of each disciplines workflows and allows for greater communication across disciplines, "managers who spend a long time in one type of position usually have more difficulty in adapting to the requirements of a different type of position." (Yukl, G 2008).

However, this is not the only idea I came up with. A shorter idea, and focusing more on a hard skill within Unity, is to develop my UI skills. Creating a non-static Splash screen for a small game, with more advanced elements that simple button navigation. I am less sure of this idea, given how small scale it is, and it being less catered to the skills I wish to develop in this Master's course.

Another idea I have had is to have a look at upgrading the Scrum Poker Card activity. Looking into the cards themselves, the weighting or instruction manuals how to 'play' or even redesigning it all. This would enable me to create an artefact of a redesigned Scrum Poker set, with adjoining research into the Agile Framework.

The last idea I had was developing a Template for designers to give Artists. In it would outline style, file size and other details that artists would typically consider before beginning a project. Similar to the Twine idea, it would give me an opportunity to explore the pipelines and workflows of another discipline which I am not familiar with.

Overall, with this week I have begun to reflect on my output as a leader in the early stages of development of a project, in order to do more work now, to reduce the work for members of my team later down the line. I have also began looking into various papers on project leadership, in order to bolster my knowledge. One such paper was "The Importance of Flexible Leadership" by G. Yukl. (Yukl, G. 2008) discussing the various studies on flexibility in leadership. The paper made note of the level of seniority in all studies, outlining that those at CO or CEO levels had similarities and differences to Line Manager's. This paper was particularly apt given the section on changing situations and new contexts for a projects life, which when discussing game development, often has quite fluid and chaotic shifts in projections and direction.
